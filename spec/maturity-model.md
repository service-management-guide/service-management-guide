# Shared maturity model

Every topic's `## Maturity model` section uses this same five-level scale, applied to that topic's subject. Do not rename the levels or change their order.

| Level | Name | What it looks like |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Initiate** | The team recognizes the practice but has no consistent way of doing it. Work is ad hoc, depends on individuals, and is mostly undocumented. |
| 2 | **Develop** | Some teams have working practices, but they differ from team to team. Early tools, templates, and habits exist but are not shared or expected. |
| 3 | **Standardize** | A common, lightweight approach is written down and used across teams. Roles are clear. The approach is a starting point that teams may tailor with a stated reason. |
| 4 | **Manage** | Outcomes are measured (for example with flow metrics, service levels, or customer feedback) and reviewed. Gaps lead to improvement work that is planned and resourced. |
| 5 | **Orchestrate** | The practice is coordinated across teams, suppliers, and customers, and is adapted continually as conditions change. Improvement is routine and comes from everyone, not only from a central group. |

A topic's maturity table has 3–5 rows. Each row is a sub-dimension of the topic (for example, for Topic 3.2 — Incident Management, the rows might be "detection and triage," "major incident response," and "learning from incidents"), with a short phrase per level per row, not a repeat of this page's generic descriptions.

The scale is a conversation tool for teams, not a certification. It is not the same as the process maturity scales in ITIL, ISO/IEC 33000, or CMMI, and a team should not claim a score from those frameworks on the strength of this one.

For AI and automation topics, rows usually include oversight (who reviews and approves), evidence (audit trail and measured outcomes), and safety (rollback, limits, and kill switch).
