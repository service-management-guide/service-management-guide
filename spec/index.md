# Service Management Guide — specification

This is the governing specification for the *Service Management Guide*. Where any file in this monorepo disagrees with this document, this document wins: fix the file, or change this document on purpose and bring every affected artifact into line in the same change (see §8).

This repository uses specification-driven development: the spec is written first, `plan.md` says how it will be built, `tasks.md` tracks the build, and every artifact (topic, skill, script, site page) traces back to a section here.

## 1. Product definition

- **Working title:** Service Management Guide
- **Subtitle:** A practical handbook for service management in the age of ITIL 5: agile, AI, automation, and analytics, with research, practices, examples, team conversations, AI skills, and AI prompts.
- **Companion:** the *Service Management Metrics* book (`~/git/service-management-metrics/service-management-metrics`) holds the measures; this guide holds the practices. Topics here summarize and point there for metric definitions.
- **Lineage:** this guide generalizes the *Agile ITIL Guide* (`~/git/agile-itil-guide/agile-itil-guide`). It keeps that guide's stack, tooling, and discipline, and changes the centre of gravity from "agile plus ITIL 4" to service management as ITIL 5 now frames it: digital product and service management, AI governance, and measurable value.
- **Format:** Markdown topics, one directory per topic under `locales/<locale>/topics/<slug>/`, stitched together by `README.md`, `glossary/index.md`, and `index/index.md`, and published as a website by the `service-management-guide.github.io/` subproject.
- **Premise:** four forces now shape service management, and every topic looks at its subject through them:
  1. **Agile**: continuous delivery, continuous stakeholder involvement, continuous improvement, ongoing evolution, sustainable pace, and motivated people.
  2. **AI**: assistants, virtual agents, AIOps, generative and agentic AI, and the governance, risk, and human oversight they need.
  3. **Automation**: workflows, pipelines, infrastructure as code, self-service, and auto-remediation, chosen deliberately and built as products.
  4. **Analytics**: data foundations, observability, forecasting, process analytics, and honest measurement.
- **ITIL 5:** ITIL (Version 5) was introduced by PeopleCert in 2026. This guide treats ITIL 4 concepts as the widely documented foundation and ITIL 5 as the direction: see §6 item 6 for the verification rules.
- **Centre of gravity:** worldwide and vendor-neutral. Commercial companies, the public sector, nonprofits, and managed-service providers are first-class readers. Service management is wider than IT: Topic 1.9 — Enterprise Service Management covers human resources, facilities, and legal. Named frameworks and tools are examples of patterns, not recommendations.
- **Audience:** delivery teams, service desk and operations staff, product owners and service owners, agile coaches, automation and AI engineers, data and analytics practitioners, IT and service managers, CIOs, and anyone preparing for ITIL or agile certification.
- **Distinctive features:** every topic includes discussion questions, a fictional worked example, an "Agile, AI, automation, and analytics" lens, AI prompts to copy and paste, and links to matching AI skills in `skills/`.

## 5. Voice, style, and formatting

Full contract in `STYLE_GUIDE.md`. Key points:

- Practical, calm, and plain. Second person for guidance; third person for describing practice. About 2,500–3,500 words per topic, reached by depth.
- Expand acronyms on first use in each topic. Cross-references carry number and title: "(see Topic 3.4 — Change Enablement)".
- Neutral between frameworks, and neutral about AI: name the benefit, the risk, and the trade-off. No hype words (revolutionary, game-changing, cutting-edge, seamless, disruptive, best-in-class).
- **Agile stance.** Never write "agile delivery"; write "continuous delivery". De-emphasize sprint, Scrum, and Kanban; emphasize continuous stakeholder involvement, continuous improvement, ongoing evolution, sustainable pace, and motivated people. Mention the three only where a topic is about them or a source names them, and conditionally ("If your team chooses to use sprints").
- **AI stance.** AI assists people and is accountable to people. Every AI use case names an owner, a human oversight point, and how errors are caught. Never present AI output as verified fact.
- **Automation stance.** Automate what is stable, frequent, and well understood; improve the process first. Every automation names an owner, a rollback, and an audit trail.
- **Analytics stance.** Measure to learn and decide, not to rank people. Name the data source and its limits. Metrics describe teams, services, and systems.

## 6. Grounding and citation rules (non-negotiable)

1. **Never invent sources:** no made-up citations, URLs, statistics, survey figures, or quotations. If a figure cannot be verified, describe the pattern without the number.
2. **Verify every Wikipedia link** before linking it. 5–12 Wikipedia links per topic, each also listed in `## References`.
3. **Core research sources:** Wikipedia articles on ITIL, IT service management, IT asset management, Agile software development, DevOps, Site reliability engineering, AIOps, and Artificial intelligence; then their primary sources.
4. **Preferred bodies of knowledge:** the Agile Manifesto; the Scrum Guide and Kanban Guide; ITIL publications from the owner (summarize, never reproduce); ISO/IEC 20000, 19770, 27001, and 42001 (cite by name and scope); the NIST AI Risk Management Framework; the DORA research program; Google's SRE books; Lean and DevOps literature.
5. **The worked example** must be clearly fictional: a generic type of organization.
6. **ITIL versions.** Concepts from ITIL 4 (service value system, guiding principles, four dimensions, service value chain, practices) remain the widely documented foundation. Always label version-specific statements ("in ITIL 4", "in ITIL 5"). ITIL 5 was introduced by PeopleCert in 2026. As of October 2026 the verified position, from PeopleCert's own certification pages, is limited to: it is described as "digital product and service management" including the impact of AI and emerging technologies; it stresses "value-focused practice" linking strategy, delivery, and measurable outcomes; an ITIL Foundation Bridge (Version 5) exists for ITIL 4 holders. Everything else (practice names, structure, percentages of new content, dates) is unconfirmed until read on peoplecert.org. Third-party training blogs are not sufficient sources.
7. **Version-sensitive claims** (ITIL, ISO/IEC standards, AI regulation such as the EU AI Act, certification names and prices) are dated in the text and checked at write time. The guide is not legal advice.
8. **Trademarks:** ITIL® is a registered trademark of its owner; SAFe®, Scrum, and other names belong to their owners. `README.md` carries one trademark notice.

## 9. Website subproject: `service-management-guide.github.io/`

- **Design system:** Lily Design System packages (`@lilydesignsystem/svelte-headless`, `svelte-picker-bar`, `themes`); headless components, all styling in `static/assets/style.css`; institution-specific themes excluded by `scripts/sync-themes.sh`.

- **Routes:** `/` redirects to the reader's locale; `/<locale>/`; `/<locale>/topics/<slug>/`; `/glossary/`; `/index/`; `/skills/`; `/sitemap.xml`. Every route prerenders.
- **Content:** `scripts/sync-content.sh` copies locales, glossary, index, skills, `spec/topics.tsv`, and `llms.*` into `src/content/` and `static/`. The copy is committed; never edit it by hand.
- **Deployment:** `bin/deploy-site` runs `git subtree push --prefix=service-management-guide.github.io site main` to the `service-management-guide/service-management-guide.github.io` repository; its `.github/workflows/deploy.yml` builds with pnpm and deploys with `actions/deploy-pages`. Never push to the site repository directly.
- **Quality bar:** `pnpm check` reports 0 errors; `pnpm build` has no prerender failures; no horizontal scroll at 1440px and 390px; a clean console.

## 11. Review and quality gates

- **Structural gate:** template sections, counts, symlinks. **Link gate:** Wikipedia links resolve and appear in References; skill links resolve. **Source gate:** nothing invented; no long reproduction of framework text. **Consistency gate:** titles, cross-references, en-001 conventions, peer ids.
- `bin/check` automates the structural gate and the offline parts of the link and consistency gates. `bin/check --links` resolves Wikipedia slugs; `--strict` fails on stubs.
- Report findings as `file — gate — section — what's wrong — what passing looks like`, most severe first.

Decide the final manifest first; commit before bulk changes; change §4, `spec/topics.tsv`, README, index, glossary, and cross-references from one old→new table in one pass; run `bin/check`. A renamed slug moves the directory with its `.locale-peer-id` unchanged.
